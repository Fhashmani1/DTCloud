Understanding CMMC

What CMMC Requires, Level by Level

The Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification (CMMC) is the Department of Defense's framework for verifying that contractors and subcontractors in the Defense Industrial Base protect Federal Contract Information (FCI) and Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI). It's no longer a planning exercise — the DFARS rule tying CMMC to contract award took effect on November 10, 2025.

Enforcement is phasing in over several years: today, many contracts still allow self-assessment, but that changes on November 10, 2026, when a passed Level 2 C3PAO certification becomes the standard requirement for contracts involving CUI. Waiting until a solicitation requires it is a real risk — assessment slots are already booking out months in advance.

  • Level 1 – Foundational: 17 basic practices, FCI only, annual self-assessment
  • Level 2 – Advanced: 110 NIST SP 800-171 practices, CUI, self-assessment or C3PAO certification depending on the contract
  • Level 3 – Expert: adds NIST SP 800-172 practices, government-led DIBCAC assessment

FAQ

CMMC Compliance Questions, Answered

What is CMMC, and why does it matter now?

CMMC is the DoD's framework for verifying that contractors protect Federal Contract Information and Controlled Unclassified Information. Since November 10, 2025, it has been tied directly to contract award through the DFARS rule — meaning a missing certification can now cost you the bid, not just invite a future audit.

Which CMMC level applies to my business?

It depends on what data you handle. Level 1 covers Federal Contract Information with a lighter, self-assessed set of 17 practices. Level 2 covers Controlled Unclassified Information and requires all 110 NIST SP 800-171 practices, with a growing number of contracts requiring third-party C3PAO certification. Level 3 adds practices for the most sensitive programs, assessed directly by the government.

Do I need a C3PAO assessment, or can I self-assess?

Today, many Level 2 contracts still allow self-assessment. That changes on November 10, 2026, when Phase 2 makes a passed C3PAO certification the standard requirement for contracts involving CUI. If your pipeline includes CUI work, it's worth assuming certification will be required.

What does a CMMC gap assessment involve?

We evaluate your current controls, policies, and documentation against the 110 practices required at Level 2 (or the relevant practice set for your target level), then hand you a prioritized Plan of Action and Milestones that shows exactly what to fix first.

How long does certification take?

It varies by starting point, but most organizations need six to twelve months to close control gaps and build required documentation — and assessment slots with certified C3PAOs are booking further out as the Phase 2 deadline approaches. Starting early protects your timeline.

How does DTCloudOn help with the whole process?

We handle gap assessment, remediation, documentation, and pre-assessment prep, backed by RPO partnership for guidance grounded in current Cyber-AB standards — so you walk into your official assessment prepared instead of guessing.
