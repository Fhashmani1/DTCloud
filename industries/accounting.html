Regulatory Compliance

Data Security and Compliance Support for Accounting Firms

Accounting firms handle sensitive financial records, tax information, and confidential client data. DTCloudOn helps organizations implement technical safeguards, including access controls, data retention, audit logging, endpoint protection, and secure collaboration tools, to support applicable security and compliance requirements.

For most tax and accounting practices, the key requirements are the FTC Safeguards Rule, which applies to tax preparers as financial institutions under the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act, and the IRS guidance in Publication 4557, Safeguarding Taxpayer Data. Both call for a written information security plan (WISP), multi-factor authentication, encryption, and an incident response plan. We put those technical controls in place and help you document them.

  • Data retention policies and audit trail configuration
  • Identity management and role-based access controls
  • Secure storage and protection of confidential financial information
  • Security monitoring and compliance reporting tools

In most cases, yes. The FTC treats tax preparers and many accounting firms as financial institutions under the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act, which means they must maintain a written information security program. The rule's requirements include a designated qualified individual, risk assessments, multi-factor authentication, encryption of customer information, and an incident response plan. Firms should confirm how the rule applies to their practice with their own legal or compliance advisor.

A WISP, or Written Information Security Plan, is a document describing how a firm protects taxpayer data, including its safeguards, the staff responsible, and its breach response steps. The IRS expects paid tax preparers to have one, and IRS Publication 5708 provides a template. DTCloudOn helps firms put the technical safeguards described in a WISP into practice.

Accounting firms are frequent targets of phishing, business email compromise, ransomware, credential theft, and data exfiltration because of the volume of financial and personal information they hold. Attacks often increase during tax season. Proactive security services reduce exposure through continuous monitoring, staff awareness, and preventive controls.
