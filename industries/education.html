Frequently Asked Questions About IT for Schools

Education IT services cover the management, security, and support of the networks, devices, and applications used for teaching, learning, and administration. They keep classroom technology working, protect sensitive student data, and reduce technical disruptions that interrupt instruction.

The main federal law is FERPA, the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act, which protects the privacy of student education records at schools that receive federal funding. Many states add their own student privacy laws, and apps used with children under 13 must also follow COPPA. IT services support these obligations with access controls, encryption, monitored backups, vendor reviews, and incident response plans, while the school remains responsible for its own policies.

The Children's Internet Protection Act, or CIPA, requires schools and libraries that receive E-Rate discounts for internet access to adopt an internet safety policy and use technology that blocks or filters obscene and harmful content for minors. In practice, most schools use a web content filter that covers student devices both on campus and at home, with reporting for administrators.

E-Rate is the FCC program, administered by USAC, that provides discounts to schools and libraries for internet access and internal network equipment such as Wi-Fi access points, switches, and cabling. Schools must follow E-Rate's competitive bidding rules. DTCloudOn can help schools assess their network, plan eligible upgrades, and install and support the equipment.
