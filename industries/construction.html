Defense Contractor Cybersecurity

CMMC Readiness and Cybersecurity for Defense Construction Contractors

Construction companies supporting Department of Defense projects may be subject to cybersecurity requirements based on their contracts and the information they handle. Organizations processing, storing, or transmitting Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI) may need to implement security requirements aligned with NIST SP 800-171 and CMMC Level 2.

DTCloudOn helps defense contractors and subcontractors prepare for applicable cybersecurity requirements through readiness planning, security control implementation, Microsoft 365 security solutions, access management, and technical remediation. We help organizations identify technology gaps and develop practical plans to strengthen their cybersecurity environments.

On-Site Services Nationwide

On-Site IT for Job Sites, Trailers, and Construction Offices

Construction IT does not stay in one building. Every new project needs a working network on a site that often has no internet, power that comes and goes, and equipment exposed to weather and theft. DTCloudOn sends technicians to job sites and offices in all 50 states to get crews connected fast, then monitors those connections remotely until the project closes out.

At the job site

  • Job-site trailer and field office setup. Networks, computers, printers and phones installed in construction trailers and temporary field offices.
  • Job-site internet connectivity. Cellular, fixed wireless and satellite internet with automatic failover for sites without wired service.
  • Outdoor Wi-Fi and wireless bridges. Weather-rated access points and point-to-point links that extend the network across the site and between trailers.
  • Job-site security cameras. IP cameras with remote viewing to watch equipment, materials and progress after hours.
  • Access control. Badge readers and door access for trailers, tool rooms and secured areas.
  • Rugged tablet and field device rollout. Rugged tablets, laptops and phones prepared, enrolled and handed out to crews.
  • Mobilization and demobilization. IT set up at project start, then removed, inventoried and redeployed when the job closes out.
  • On-site troubleshooting. Technician visits for network, device and connectivity problems that cannot be fixed remotely.

At the main and branch offices

  • Structured cabling and network build-outs. Ethernet cabling, switches, firewalls, server racks and UPS power.
  • Plotter and wide-format printer setup. Plan printing in the office and in field trailers.
  • VoIP phone systems. Business phones for main offices, field offices and trailers.
  • Office security cameras and access control. Cameras, badge readers and door access for offices and equipment yards.

Frequently Asked Questions About IT for Construction Companies

Construction companies typically need managed IT services, reliable internet and networks at job sites and trailers, mobile device management, data backup, and cybersecurity protection. These services support project coordination, file sharing, and real-time communication between the office and field crews.

Managed IT support keeps communication reliable between the office and job sites through secure networks, mobile device management, and monitored systems. Plans, schedules, and instructions can be shared quickly and securely without interruptions.

Most job sites have no wired internet, so connectivity usually comes from cellular, fixed wireless, or satellite service, often with a backup connection that takes over if the main one drops. Weather-rated Wi-Fi access points and point-to-point wireless bridges then extend the network to trailers, staging areas, and the building itself. DTCloudOn installs this on-site and monitors it remotely for the life of the project.

Construction firms handle sensitive project data, contracts, bank details for large payments, and, in some cases, government-related information. Cybersecurity services help protect against ransomware, data breaches, and unauthorized access that could delay projects or expose confidential information.

Contractors are frequent targets of business email compromise, where criminals pose as a supplier or subcontractor and send fake payment instructions. The best defenses combine email security filtering, multi-factor authentication on email accounts, alerts for lookalike domains, and a firm rule that any change to bank details is confirmed by phone using a known number before money is sent.

IT services support mobile crews through device management, secure remote access, and protected connections that let workers open files and applications from job sites. This improves coordination, reduces delays, and keeps workflows consistent in the field.

Data backup and recovery services protect critical project files, blueprints, and documentation from loss due to system failures, cyber incidents, or accidental deletion. Reliable recovery options minimize downtime and prevent costly project disruptions.
